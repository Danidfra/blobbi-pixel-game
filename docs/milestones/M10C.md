## What changed, and why

### 1. One active island (onboarding)
*Why:* the first public release gives each player one Carrier and island; the title exposed island creation too
directly. A new player sees **BLOBBI PIXEL**, "a little world awaits you.", **BEGIN** and **SETTINGS** (ABOUT as a
small row); BEGIN leads to the unchanged Carrier choice, naming and creation cinematic. A returning player sees
"welcome back!", **CONTINUE** (the island's name, Carrier and day) and **SETTINGS**, with ISLANDS and ABOUT as small
rows — no NEW ISLAND.

This is a product rule only. Every island keeps its own identity and save. The active island is remembered explicitly
(`blobbi-pixel-lab:active-world`, falling back to the most recent). The ISLANDS page lists every island the browser
holds — older builds could make several — marks the ACTIVE one, opens (and so re-activates) any of them, and deletes
only after asking. Nothing is deleted or merged. `#islands=many` brings NEW ISLAND back for development.

### 2. Growth in the story
*Why:* players did not know the island grows, and growth is the way to the sea. **"Help your island grow"** is a
chapter-3 main quest after "Something that hums" (the stone hums: the island is alive) and before "Listen to the
Carrier", which now requires it. Its goal box counts the growth requirements met (1/7 …); growth (L) shows them; the
existing "your island can grow" tip still points to the panel. Growth is never instant: the stage-1 requirements are
unchanged (the first home quest, harmony, care, harvests, crafting, a find, two days). Its reward is a journal note
("A growing island"). Because the rod, the seal and the canoe all come after listening to the Carrier, the shore is
reachable by the time any of them matters.

### 3. Guaranteed ocean access (the landing)
*Why:* before, a manta's island never reached the sea (20–50 px of its body lies between land and water), and turtles
and whales did on some seeds only. Now it is an invariant of generation: from stage 1, every island of every Carrier,
for every seed, has a **landing** — a short tongue of shore across the Carrier's edge margin to open water, chosen from
the world's geometry: carrying surface only, never a moving part or protected anatomy, the canoe fits beyond it clear of
the moving parts, can paddle out to open sea, and the Blobbi lands back there. Natural objects on its ground are
cleared for good (indices kept). See [ocean access](../specifications/OCEAN-ACCESS.md) for the full rules.

**Carrier-specific shoreline rules:** turtle — on a flank of the shell, away from the head, neck, flippers and tail;
whale — between the blowholes and the knuckles, clear of the pectoral fins and flukes; manta — off its head-to-tail
axis (cephalic fins in front, tail behind), clear of the beating wings.

Growth version 4 = version 3 plus the landing (same objects, same land). Islands grown before are upgraded on load and
told once: "The shore: a path to the sea has opened!". The landing is a place on the map. Before the sea is reachable,
a cast or a launch at a far sea says "the ocean is still out of reach" (a notice at most once a minute: "help your
island grow to open a path to the shore!"), and so does the canoe recipe.

Evidence: `tests/ocean-access.test.ts` (6 seeds × 3 Carriers × stages 1 and 4); `scripts/m10c-access-scan.ts`, 30 seeds
per Carrier: the landing works at stages 1 and 4 for 30/30 turtles, 30/30 mantas and 30/30 whales; no island reaches
the sea at stage 0.

### 4. Resource regeneration: audit and changes
What actually happens (in world time, which passes only while playing; never while the game is closed; each
resource's timer saved): a felled tree is a stump for 20 s, its ground clear for 600 s, then — if the spot is free — a
seedling grows to the full tree in 240 s (a seedling at ~10 min 20 s, a tree at ~14 min 20 s: the "15 minutes" the owner
saw). A broken rock: rubble 20 s, clear 900 s. Bushes 480 s, mushrooms 600 s, small rocks 900 s, natural stumps never;
sticks, stones and seeds 120–180 s. A resource returns only to a free spot (never under a building, path, rug,
furniture, doorway, plot, crop, planted tree, the Blobbi or the canoe, never cutting a way) and waits otherwise.

The timings stay (they keep materials generous; driftwood remains the wood fallback). What changed is what was
annoying: young growth was untouchable, and ground had to be cleared again and again.

### 5. Clearing during growth
Every stage can be removed with one swing: a stump (nothing), a **seedling** (nothing; it is stepped over), a **young
tree** (one log), **rubble** (nothing), and a returning rock now **forms** — a small deposit (one stone), a growing rock
(two stones), then whole over 6 minutes. Clearing early restarts the full wait, so it never out-earns waiting (felling
gives 2–4 wood, breaking 3–5 stones); quests count only real fellings and breakings. Footprints follow the stage, and
nothing is built, laid or tilled on a seedling or a forming rock ("something is growing there").

### 6. Prepared land
The least complex mechanism that fits: a **clearing flag** (2 sticks + 1 fibre, by hand, known from the start), an
ordinary placeable — saved in the island frame (so it survives growth), walked under (it never blocks a path), moved
with M, picked up into the bag. Within its circle (30 px) no wild thing returns; picked up, nature may return. Its
circle shows as dots on the ground nearby and while arranging. No zoning UI.

### 7. Planted trees (KI-49)
Trees the player plants (P) are saved (`planted`: island frame, world time, seed, size), keep growing in world time
across reloads, growth and migration, block once rooted, keep resources off their spot, and can be dug up with the axe
(no wood: planting is free, so a yield would be an exploit).

### 8. Houses and building
Kept: building on cleared land, the expansion advice and its arrows. New: nothing is built over regrowth; a planted
tree in the way of an upgrade can now really be removed (the advice already said "axe"); a clearing flag near the house
is named ("too close to the clearing flag") and can be picked up. Nothing is ever deleted or moved automatically.

### 9. Side quest tracking
One active side quest can be pinned from the journal (PIN, or P) to a small SIDE card under the main goal, at every
zoom, on desktop and phone; UNPIN removes it; it comes off by itself when done; saved (`pinnedQuest`).

### 10. Locked recipe hints
A recipe whose teaching quest is open shows LOCKED with `Complete "<quest>" to unlock.` (from the quest's own data:
no quest text in the UI) and cannot be made. Recipes of quests not yet open, and of discovery quests, stay a surprise.

### 11. Canoe and sea polish
KI-50: one hull profile in every heading (fuller stern, sharper bow, rising ends, planks along it, a stem, a V prow
toward the viewer). KI-51: the sea's edge is rounded and breathes, drawn as an ordered-dither darkening under whitecaps
and rough all the way beyond — no straight line. KI-52: a moored canoe can be called back into the bag from anywhere
ashore (CALL IT BACK), never while aboard; still one canoe. KI-53: every fish has its own silhouette.

### 12. Save reliability
Schema 12 adds `planted`, `plantedSerial` and `pinnedQuest`; records 1–11 migrate. A record from a newer version of
the game, or one that cannot be read, is never overwritten (KI-02): it is copied aside, the world plays without saving
and says why. A failed write (storage full) never stops play, is said once, and is retried. See
[save and persistence](../architecture/SAVE-AND-PERSISTENCE.md).

### 13. Nostr readiness (assessment only)
[Nostr persistence readiness](../architecture/NOSTR-PERSISTENCE-READINESS.md) records the record's boundaries
(identity and generation, world modifications, progress, companion, inventory), what already helps (stable identity,
regenerable worlds, ~1.5–9 KB snapshots, versioned migrations, safe failure, owner separation), the gaps (no change
tracking, identity not owner-bound, last-writer-wins, local-only inventory) and the open decisions. No protocol is
proposed as established; no infrastructure was added without a concrete need.

## Performance
Back to back against phase B on one development machine ([details](../testing/perf/README.md#m10-phase-c-the-road-to-the-ocean)):
headless frame times are level within noise in every scene except the canoe by the sea's corner (+0.3–0.5 ms: the
rough water is now a rounded, fading ring); a first version that repainted all the water beyond the sea every frame
(up to 17 ms at 1920×1080) was found and fixed before committing. Loading a grown island costs 150–300 ms more (planning
the landing, the upgrade), and the Carrier's animation frames are now built during loading instead of on the first
frame (a whale: load + first frame 3.63 → 3.94 s). Chrome at 4× throttling (a proxy, not a real low-end device): FPS
level, no new long tasks. Unthrottled Chrome: 60 FPS, no long tasks, every scene, desktop and phone. **No real
low-end device was measured.**
