## What M10 phase A changes

1. **Listening to the Carrier after growth.** The listening spot was found once on the stage-0 island and never
   moved, so after growth it could be far inland, under new trees or behind a house. It is now derived from the
   island as it is (current collision and reachability): from the head toward the island, the first ground the
   Blobbi can stand on and reach; where that is taken, the nearest free, reachable ground by that shore where,
   facing the head, nothing else answers E first. Recomputed when the island's collision or structures change; the
   head place moves with it; untouched islands keep their M9 spot and places exactly.
2. **Fishing rod.** New rod art (cork grip, reel, tapering blank, pale tip) with fishing orientations (forward and
   up, down toward the viewer, up over the head facing away); every orientation marks its tip. A cast animation
   (wind back, swing, hold) with the float flying from the tip on an arc. The line is drawn at screen resolution
   from the rod's tip as drawn, in the rod's layer — behind the Blobbi exactly when the rod is — slack while
   waiting, taut on a bite. [Gameplay systems](../game-design/GAMEPLAY-SYSTEMS.md#fishing).
3. **Seamless ocean.** The flat margin round a zoomed-out world is gone: the intro cinematic's open sea (moved to
   `src/render/open-sea.ts`, shared) paints the terrain painter's deep water and the live renderer's waves beyond the
   edge, in tiles made once. No seam on any Carrier, at any zoom, while the camera moves.
4. **Performance.** A measured baseline, frame-only profiles, then pixel-identical fixes for what was expensive
   (a wasted Blobbi rasterization on every new pose, a garbage-heavy rasterizer loop, per-pixel lighting maths,
   per-frame allocations). [Methodology, baseline and results](../testing/perf/README.md).
5. **A fourth camera mode, up close (6×).** Evaluated against 8× (too tight on a sideways phone). Whole blocks, the
   Blobbi natively at 72 px, the existing jitter-free follow, world and room bounds, a compact HUD (needs chip and
   time; prompts and notices stay — **replaced in phase B by the original HUD at every zoom**, at the owner's request), remembered with the other modes; the camera indicator is a click/tap target that
   cycles the modes (touch players had no camera control).
6. **Marine life.** Fish schools, jellyfish (glowing at night), occasional dolphins, a curious seal (recurring) and a
   rare glowing ray (night only) — a pure function of the world seed and world time, never simulated or saved, a
   handful at once. E waves to the seal (it rolls over) or watches the ray; first meetings are discoveries (a
   keepsake and a journal note); waves count toward the new side quest *Sea friends*.
7. **Future architecture.** Extension points for boats, free navigation, travel, fishing regions and diving:
   [The ocean, later](../architecture/OCEAN-FUTURE.md) (DESIGNED ONLY).

## Performance in one paragraph

Unthrottled Chrome on the development machine: 60 FPS and no long tasks in every scene, every camera mode, at
1280×720 and 844×390. With 4× CPU throttling as a **proxy** for a slower machine (not a measurement of one), the
turtle overviews at 1280×720 went from 21–38 FPS (with long tasks at stage 4) to 38–58 FPS (none), and every
scene stays above 30 FPS. The open sea costs about 1 ms more a frame in a 1920×1080 overview. Details and every raw run:
[docs/testing/perf](../testing/perf/README.md).

world; marine creatures are small world-pixel sprites.
