## What phase B changes

1. **The HUD** is the original one at every camera zoom (phase A's compact up-close HUD is gone); its layout adapts to
   the screen, never to the zoom.
2. **Land clearing** ([spec](../specifications/LAND-CLEARING.md)): terrain is permanent, resources are temporary.
   Felling, breaking or clearing frees the ground for building; bushes, small rocks and natural stumps or mushrooms can
   now be cleared too. Resources come back on their own spot, gradually, **only when it is free** (never over a
   building, path, plot, crop, doorway, planted tree or the Blobbi, never cutting the way to a door) — otherwise they
   wait; nothing ever moves. Driftwood keeps wood within reach on an island built over.
3. **House expansion** ([housing](../specifications/HOUSING.md)): the HOME panel says what is in the way, where and with
   what ("Clear the tree to the left of your house to make room (axe)."), and marks it in the world; when the land
   itself is in the way it says where a move would give room. Paths and rugs no longer refuse a bigger house. Every
   test island of every Carrier can grow its first house (at once, after clearing, or after the suggested move).
4. **Fishing progression**: the line's reach is unchanged and no dock is forced; sea fishing comes with growth (turtle
   from stage 1, manta from 2, whale from 3 on the test seeds), and a cast at a sea too far says so.
5. **The canoe** ([spec](../specifications/CANOE.md)): made at a workbench after *Gone fishing*, launched from the
   shore, boarded with a hop, paddled freely (keys or the touch stick) on the sea round the Carrier — never over land,
   its body or its moving parts — inside a region past the world's edge whose rough-water edge and current turn it
   back; landed on reachable shore; saved aboard or moored; never stranded.
6. **Fishing from the canoe** reuses `fish()`; the line still leaves the rod's tip.
7. **Fishing variety**: flounder (coast, day), mackerel (open water), moonfish (open water, night); a fish log
   (journal FISH tab); mackerel skewers and kelp soup.
8. **Ocean discoveries**: a bottle adrift somewhere new each day until found, the kelp garden (seaweed), the silver
   shoal at dawn and dusk — never missable, each a discovery with a journal note.
9. **KI-34 fixed**: the world-generation budget times the test process's CPU, best of three, not the wall clock.

## Save compatibility

Save schema 11 ([save and persistence](../architecture/SAVE-AND-PERSISTENCE.md)): `trees[].regrowAt`,
`rocks[].reformAt`, `cleared`, `boat`, `caught`. Older records load unchanged: their felled trees and broken rocks keep
the old timelines exactly; no canoe; nothing cleared. World generation is unchanged (pinned resource and object
signatures still pass); no terrain or construction moves.

## Performance

No regression against phase A, measured back to back (headless and Chrome); unthrottled Chrome holds 60 FPS with no
long tasks in every scene including the canoe; 4× CPU throttling (a proxy, not a measurement of real hardware) keeps
every scene at 58–60 FPS. Details and raw runs: [docs/testing/perf](../testing/perf/README.md#m10-phase-b-coastal-life-the-canoe).
