`develop`, starting point `7e0f278` (M11D.4; the six earlier local commits preserved). Island save schema 12

## 1. A Baby became an Adult when the island grew (ROOT CAUSE FOUND, FIXED)

**Cause.** Island growth (LET THE ISLAND GROW, and the dev stage tools) rebuilds the live world from its save
(`src/sim/regrow.ts`, `rebuildFromSave`): the stage is saved, then a NEW `LiveWorld` is built exactly as a reload
would. That constructor was called with the playground's Blobbi source and size but **without the life stage**, and
`LiveWorld` defaults to the adult. So every growth swapped the walking Baby for the adult of the same genome. A reload
read the stage back from the selection record (`blobbi-pixel-lab:selected-blobbi`), which is why the Baby "came back".
Nothing canonical was ever changed: the selection, the guest record and the Nostr events all still said Baby; only the
rebuilt world's in-memory Blobbi was wrong.

**Fix (the state transition, not the picture).** The rebuild now carries the OLD world's own Blobbi into the new one:
its source, its size and its life stage (`old.blobbi.source`, `old.blobbi.size`, `stage: old.blobbi.identity.stage`).
The `blobbi`/`blobbiSize` options are ignored (kept for callers) so no caller can swap or "grow up" the Blobbi through
a rebuild; the playground no longer passes them. Growth belongs to the Carrier and the island; it never changes who
walks or their stage, and nothing in the game evolves a Blobbi on its own.

**Tests** (`tests/m11d5.test.ts`): a Baby stays the same Baby (seed, source, sprite height) through growth stages 1, 2
and 3 and after a reload; old callers passing another Blobbi cannot swap it; an adult stays the same adult; walking,
quests and the clock never change the stage. The two growth tests FAIL with the old line (checked by reverting it).
Browser: a new guest Baby, `devGrow(1)` and `devGrow(2)`: still `stage: "baby"`, same `d`.

## 2. The Baby's shadow (FIXED)

The Blobbi's shadow was drawn from its gameplay footprint (`footRx`), which a Baby shares with the adult on purpose
(collision and paths must not change with the stage). Now `BlobbiEntity.shadowRx` is the DRAWN shadow's half width:
the footprint for an adult (unchanged, pixel for pixel), the Baby's own rendered body for a Baby (its front sprite's
foot, never wider than the footprint). `footRx`/`footRy` are untouched, so collision, pathfinding, reach and doors are
exactly as before. Measured: adult foot/shadow 3/3, Baby 3/2 (three seeds); in the browser 4/2 and 3/2.

Sheet: `examples/m11d5/shadow-adult-baby-grown-by-zoom.png` (rows: adult, Baby, Baby after growth; columns: overview,
exploration, close-up, up close). The interior renderer draws no Blobbi shadow (unchanged). Walking and hopping use the
same shadow (the hop lifts the sprite, the shadow stays).

## 3. Naming a new Blobbi after its birth (IMPLEMENTED)

Right after the birth, once the Blobbi stands on its island (no menu, no cinematic, present, no speech bubble; about a
second), the first little quest opens: **"What should we call our new friend?"** / **"Como vamos chamar nosso novo
amigo?"** / **"¿Cómo llamaremos a nuestro nuevo amigo?"** (`src/ui/screens/naming.ts`). The Blobbi stands big on a
little island beside the question, looking at the player. TYPE A NAME (the hidden input, 24 characters), THAT'S THE
NAME!, LATER. On confirm: "Momo!", "Momo loves the new name!", twinkles and hops on the card, and in the world the
Blobbi reacts (excited, three hops, the happy sound). LET'S PLAY returns to the game.

- **Stored** in the Blobbi's public record (`blobbi-pixel-lab:guest-blobbis`): `name`, plus `named: true`. No new
  identity, no key, no change of owner, `d` or look; nothing published. Survives reloads, island changes, activation
  (the account flow skips its name page for a named Blobbi) and both backups (the backup's public line carries
  `named`, restored Blobbis are not asked again).
- **Existing names are kept.** A record from before M11D.5 counts as named when it was registered or its name is not
  the suggested one (the player renamed it in M11D.4); an untouched suggested name is asked once.
- **LATER** keeps the suggested name, remembers `namingLaterAt`, says "You can choose a name any time in SETTINGS >
  ACCOUNT", and the ACCOUNT page shows **Choose a name for your new friend** while it waits. The question comes back
  once in a later session after 20 s of calm play; never twice in a session, never for a named or registered Blobbi.
- A registered Blobbi's name is part of its canonical state: it is renamed only in its home apps (unchanged rule).
- The invitation (§8) waits while a newborn's name has been neither chosen nor put off.

## 4. Two backups (IMPLEMENTED)

After LET'S DO IT (and the name page, only if needed) the flow asks **"How would you like to keep your Blobbi safe?"**
(PT-BR "Como você quer guardar a chave do seu Blobbi?", ES "¿Cómo quieres guardar la llave de tu Blobbi?") with two
illustrated cards:

| Card | Subtitle | What it makes |
|---|---|---|
| **Recovery Key** (a brass key) / Chave de recuperação / Llave de recuperación | Simple, no password / Simples, sem senha / Simple, sin contraseña | the plain NIP-19 `nsec1…` in a text file |
| **Protected Key** (a key behind a padlock, tag "Safer") / Chave protegida / Llave protegida | Extra protection with a password / Proteção extra com senha / Protección extra con contraseña | the NIP-49 `ncryptsec1…` (M11D.4) |

Neither is mandatory over the other, and they are not presented as equally safe: the Protected Key carries a "Safer"
tag and one plain line under the cards says "The Protected Key is safer if someone else finds the file." Neither is
called unbreakable (a test checks the words).

**Recovery Key page**: "This key is yours. Anyone who has it can control your account and your Blobbis. Keep it
somewhere private and never share it." Then an explicit tick box, "I understand: whoever has this key controls my
account."; SAVE MY RECOVERY KEY stays disabled until it is ticked. USE A PASSWORD INSTEAD switches to the Protected Key.

**Protected Key page**: "This key is protected by a password. Keep both the file and your password safe. If you forget
the password and lose access to your account, this backup cannot help you." Then the password twice (8+ characters).

### How the Recovery Key is handled (the export rules)

- Made **only** on SAVE MY RECOVERY KEY, after the tick: `KeyVault.exportSecretKey(pubkey)` encodes the secret with
  nostr-tools `nsecEncode` inside `withSecret` (the decrypted bytes are zeroed right after).
- The text goes straight into the download (a Blob URL revoked after a second) and the screen keeps **no** reference:
  it is never drawn, never copied to the clipboard (there is no COPY for it), never logged, never in a URL, never
  stored in localStorage or IndexedDB, never sent anywhere. A JavaScript string cannot be zeroed; its last reference is
  dropped once the download is offered (stated, not hidden).
- The file, `blobbi-nostr-recovery-key-<npub8>.nsec.txt`: a few lines in the player's language (what it is, that
  anyone holding it controls the account, how to bring the Blobbi back here, and "For other Nostr apps: the line
  starting with nsec is your private key (NIP-19). Paste it only into an app you trust, where it asks for a private key
  or nsec."), the single `nsec1…` line, and the same public `blobbi-pixel-backup v1 {…}` line as the Protected Key file
  (pubkey, Blobbi d tags, names, birth times, `named`). No new crypto format: NIP-19 as every Nostr client reads it.
- **Other clients**: any app that accepts a private key / `nsec` login (for example the Blobbi web apps, or clients
  with "log in with a private key") takes the `nsec1…` line pasted alone. The Protected Key's `ncryptsec1…` line is
  imported where a client supports NIP-49.

## 5. Verification (IMPLEMENTED, both kinds)

After SAVE: I SAVED IT: LET'S CHECK IT. The player loads the backup BACK: choose the file or paste its text (for a
Recovery Key, the key pasted alone also works). The kind is told by the text itself (`ncryptsec1…` or `nsec1…`), and
the password row appears only for a Protected Key. CHECK IT opens it and compares its public key with this identity:
only a backup that opens as this very identity counts (`backupVerifiedAt`, and since M11D.5 `backupKind` on the vault
record). A started download never counts. Errors say what happened without any secret: wrong password, damaged or not
a backup, belongs to another identity. Verification never writes an identity (it only marks the existing one). After a
good check the Blobbi on the left beams for two seconds (twinkles; still with reduced motion).

## 6. Restore (IMPLEMENTED)

"I have a secret backup" (title, ACCOUNT) accepts a Protected Key file, a Recovery Key file, or a raw `nsec` pasted
alone; the kind is detected, a password is asked only for a Protected Key. LOOK INSIDE shows, before anything is
stored: the kind, the identity (short npub), whether it is already on this device ("it will not be changed"), the
Blobbis it names (and which are already here), **"You are connected with another account. It stays connected; this
identity is only kept on this device."** when the player's connected account is a different key, and "Restoring shares
nothing and changes no island." RESTORE needs that explicit press. Rules (tested): an identity already in the vault is
never overwritten (the sealed record stays byte-identical), a Blobbi already here is never duplicated, the connected
account is never switched, no island is touched, nothing is published. A text holding both kinds, or two different
keys, is refused as unclear.

## 7. The account flow's look (IMPLEMENTED)

Every page of the flow, and the naming card, has the player's own Blobbi on the left, three times its pixel size,
standing on a little grassy island and facing the player (blinking, breathing). The words and buttons are one block,

## 11. Browser automation: why music kept playing (INVESTIGATED, FIXED FOR THE FUTURE)

**Finding.** Earlier milestones' browser checks started Google Chrome **headless** (`--headless=new`) with remote
debugging on port 9333 and throwaway profiles (`/tmp/blobbi-perf`, and folders in the session scratchpad), and
**without `--mute-audio`**. A key press sent over CDP counts as a user gesture, so the game unlocked its audio and
played its music through the computer's speakers, with no window anywhere. When a check stopped half way (an
exception), its tab was never closed, and if that Chrome was not stopped afterwards, the music kept looping. Brave and
the player's own Chrome were never used or touched by these scripts.

**State at this milestone.** Before and after the M11D.5 runs: no process with `--remote-debugging-port`, no
task-profile Chrome and no task Vite server was running (checked with `ps`, matching only the task's own flags and
profile paths); nothing needed to be terminated. Unrelated processes were not touched.

**Fix.**
- `scripts/lab-browser.mjs start|stop|status`: the one way to start Chrome for checks: headless, **`--mute-audio`**, a
  dedicated profile, port 9333; it writes what it started (`.lab-browser.json`) and `stop` closes every tab, then
  ends only processes whose command line names THAT profile directory (never another Chrome, never Brave).
- The shared harness (`scripts/m11b-browser-shots.mjs`, used by every browser script) tracks every tab it opens and
  closes them all on normal exit, on an exception, an unhandled rejection, Ctrl-C or SIGTERM; and it turns the game's
  own sound off (its audio setting, written before any page script runs) unless a check asks for sound.

## 13. Known limitations

- The Recovery Key is plaintext in a file the player keeps: anyone who finds the file controls the account (said
  plainly in the game and in the file). The game cannot zero the JavaScript string of the key; it keeps no reference.
- Keys in the vault can still be used by a script running in the page (M11D.4 threat model, KI-91, unchanged).
- The browser's download is not proof of saving: only the check counts (by design).
- A restored Blobbi comes back unregistered on the new device even if it was registered (KI-94, unchanged).
- The naming card and the flow were checked at 1280x720 and 844x390; not on a real phone keyboard (KI-95).
- The shadow is a whole-pixel ellipse: a Baby's is 2 px half width at every zoom (it scales with the world).
